Aris is the god of life and all things living, and the patron saint of benevolence in [[Revanon]]. When Aris appears in the [[Prime Material Plane]], she often appears wearing a colorful patchwork poncho common to Hashans of her time. [[Asteria|Asterians]] contribute the most worship to her name by beseeching her for healing, and believing that she will deliver them from wanton evil.

Aris is known to heal the sick and wounded when presented to her, whether on church altars, or on the beds of worshippers' homes. Individuals seeking her divine intervention construct any ritual bed they can, adorn the sick with decorative wear typically consisting of floral necklaces, flowing colorful gowns, and garnish them with an undisturbed opening to the sky. A column of light encapsulates those blessed by her benevolence, taking them from their dire strait to a finer state.
## Claim to godhood
Aris in life was a devout herald of the [[Light]], a nomad from Vinovia who traveled across Revanon to salve the wounded, and aid the fallen. Her journey began in 11 AS, when Aris led the sick out of [[Hasha]]. Recognizing the threat that the [[Brudel]] posed to the weaker city-state, Aris made the decision to evacuate as many sick and wounded from Hasha's hospitals as possible. This began a months' long march west across Vinovia, in which Aris and her gathering weathered the sands and marshes seeking refuge. While the journey was exceptionally costly, in both lives and resources, the people Aris was able to evacuate were ultimately rescued from [[The Day that Hasha Died|the temporal bubble that destroyed Hasha]]. The survivors Aris rescued, the medics who threw their lives behind her banner, and the citizens whose lives she touched in that march, formed a weatherproof contingent of Vinovian nomads. Stories about Aris were told by generation after generation, whereupon Asterians learned about her, and elevated her to godliness.

> *"Give me your tired, your poor,  
> Your huddled masses yearning to breathe free,  
> The wretched refuse of your teeming shore.  
> Send these, the homeless, tempest-tost to me,  
> I lift my lamp beside the golden door!"*
